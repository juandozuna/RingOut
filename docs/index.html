RingOut

Real-time feedback suppression for live vocal mics. An M4 iPad and a USB audio interface become a two-channel insert.

The De-Feedback result, on hardware you already carry.

View on GitHub Read the README
  1. Red A ring was caught in the last two seconds.
  2. Amber A notch is holding the ring down while the voice passes.
  3. Green The notch has released. Nothing to do.

The notch is the airbag. The model is the suspension.

Notch filters only act once a ring is already partly audible, and every notch left in place is a permanent EQ cut. A neural voice isolator keeps the voice clean but can be pushed past its limits near the edge. RingOut runs both in series, in one audio callback, with no added delay.

Detector and notch bank

Spots a ring in its first 20–40 ms and drops a 1/60-octave notch on it. Notches are temporary: they release once the ring is gone, so the steady-state count stays near zero.

Voice isolator

Separates the voice from feedback, room and noise energy the notches left behind, using the detector's findings as a hint.

Per-channel chain, in order

  1. Input conditioningDC block, 80 Hz high-pass, metering
  2. Feedback detectorUp to 8 candidate frequencies with a confidence score, every buffer
  3. Adaptive notch bankUp to 8 notches, −6 to −18 dB, released after the ring decays
  4. Voice isolatorProcesses the notched signal, guided by the detector
  5. Strength mixEqual-power blend with the dry input. 0 % is a bit-exact bypass
  6. Output limiter−0.3 dBFS, metering, bypass logic

What the current build ships. The algorithmic detector and adaptive notch bank are being built now. The neural isolator is planned; until it is trained, a DSP stand-in fills its slot so the chain, metering and Strength control can be tested end to end.

The verified rig

One named configuration that V1 is tested against. Everything except the iPad is bus-powered, and the iPad charges through the hub. It fits a 1U drawer.

iPad
iPad Pro 11" or 13" with M4 or newer, iPadOS 26 or newer
Interface
Focusrite Scarlett 4i4 4th gen (primary) or 2i2 4th gen. MOTU M4 as an alternate
Hub
USB-C hub with ≥ 85 W PD pass-through and a USB 3 data port. Avoid hubs with a built-in DAC
Power
100 W USB-C PD brick, 1.5 m cable
Cabling
Balanced throughout: TRS/XLR insert cables, plus a short loopback cable for latency measurement
Mount
1U drawer or iPad VESA rack shelf

Also supported, not verified

  • iPad Air M2/M3 and iPad Pro M1/M2: a 64-sample minimum buffer and one channel until the device passes the in-app stress test.
  • Any USB Audio Class 2 interface with at least 2 in and 2 out. Interfaces that report no latency are flagged "latency unknown".
  • Yamaha DM3/DM7 and Behringer X32/M32 USB cards work as the interface directly.

Who it's for

One shared job: more vocal level out of the PA without ringing, with minimal setup and no rack computer.

Volunteer church AV lead

150–800 seats, 2–4 vocal mics, a different operator each week. The person every design decision is checked against.

Needs One tap to start and a green, amber or red strip readable from across the booth.

Won't tolerate A settings screen mid-service, dropouts, or a license that breaks when the iPad is replaced.

Corporate AV tech

Ballrooms, lecterns, handheld Q&A mics a metre or two from front fills.

Needs Calibration under two minutes per channel, automatic recovery if a cable is pulled, and logs.

Won't tolerate Audible artifacts on speech, or a UI that needs reading glasses in the dark.

Regional and touring FOH

Clubs and festivals, fast changeovers, a different PA every night.

Needs Insert-level transparency, per-channel Strength, an instant and obvious bypass, show files per venue.

Won't tolerate More latency than the console already adds, or a box with no visibility into what it's doing.

Rental and production house

Builds 1U drawer kits of iPad, hub, interface and PD brick, rented wet or dry.

Needs Licensing that survives device swaps, a PIN lock for renters, and a diagnostics export.

Won't tolerate Fingerprint licensing that strands a unit when a battery is replaced.

Questions

Can I download it?

Not yet. RingOut V1 is in development and there is no App Store or TestFlight build. Follow the GitHub repository for progress.

Does it add latency?

The engine is designed to add zero samples. What remains is Core Audio buffering and the interface's converters, targeted at 3.0–3.8 ms round trip at a 32-sample buffer on the verified rig. The app shows the measured figure, not a computed one.

Is the neural model in the current build?

No. The current build has the algorithmic detector and adaptive notch bank, with a DSP stand-in where the isolator will go. The neural isolator is planned once training and on-device timing are proven.

Which iPads will work?

V1 is verified on iPad Pro with M4 or newer. iPad Air M2/M3 and iPad Pro M1/M2 are supported with a 64-sample minimum buffer and one channel until they pass the in-app stress test.

Does it work on instruments or choirs?

No. V1 is for vocals and speech only. Instrument and choir models are V2 candidates.

What happens if the interface is unplugged mid-show?

The design mutes output within one buffer, shows a full-screen "interface disconnected" state, and resumes automatically when the interface returns. If the audio callback stalls, a watchdog switches both channels to bit-exact bypass so the show continues unprocessed rather than going silent.

Can it fix a badly placed mic?

Only so far. If too many notches are active at once, the strip shows "loop gain too high": the system needs attention, not the app. A mic within a metre of a speaker is expected to be the hardest case.

Will it run as a plug-in in AUM or Logic?

Not in V1. V1 is a standalone app so the real-time path is fully under its control. An AUv3 version is planned for V1.x.
